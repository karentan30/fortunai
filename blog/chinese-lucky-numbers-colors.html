Chinese Lucky Numbers and Colors: The Complete Guide

By ShenYuan Research Team August 10, 2026 5 min read

Numbers and colors carry profound symbolic and energetic weight in Chinese culture — rooted not in superstition but in millennia of observation, linguistic meaning, and the principles of the five elements. Understanding which numbers and colors are considered auspicious, and why, gives you practical tools for everyday decisions: choosing a phone number, selecting business branding, decorating your home, or picking attire for an important event. This guide covers the full system — from the reasons behind each association to how you can apply them intelligently in your own life.

Lucky Numbers: Why These Digits Carry Good Fortune

8 — The Most Powerful Lucky Number

The number eight (八, bā) is the most universally auspicious number in Chinese culture, and its luck stems from both linguistic and visual sources. Phonetically, bā sounds like 发 (fā), meaning "to prosper" or "to become wealthy." Visually, the number 8 resembles the infinity symbol, suggesting endless abundance and continuous flow. In Chinese business culture, phone numbers, license plates, and addresses containing multiple 8s command premium prices. The 2008 Beijing Olympics deliberately opened at 8:08 PM on 08/08/2008 — a deliberately stacked auspicious alignment. For BaZi purposes, years, months, and dates containing the number 8 are often considered favorable for financial ventures and property transactions.

6 — The Number of Flow and Ease

Six (六, liù) sounds similar to 溜 (liū), meaning "to flow smoothly" or "all going well." In business contexts, 6 symbolizes smooth transactions, effortless progress, and harmonious relationships. The phrase "六六大顺" (liù liù dà shùn) — "everything flows perfectly" — is a common blessing, making 666 one of the luckiest number combinations in Chinese culture (quite the opposite of Western associations). Numbers like 68, 168, and 668 are especially favored in business because they combine the smoothness of 6 with the prosperity of 8.

9 — The Number of Longevity and Completion

Nine (九, jiǔ) sounds like 久 (jiǔ), meaning "long lasting" or "eternal." It is the highest single digit, representing completion, longevity, and the fullness of heaven. In imperial China, the emperor was associated with nine dragons and nine-layered palace architecture. Nine is particularly favored in matters of love and commitment — the phrase 99 roses has become the romantic standard gift in China precisely because it suggests "everlasting love." In feng shui, nine is used in activation cures: placing nine gold coins, nine plants, or nine red items in the wealth corner amplifies prosperity intention.

Unlucky Numbers to Avoid

4 — The Number of Death

Four (四, sì) sounds nearly identical to 死 (sǐ), meaning "death." This association is so strong that many Chinese hospitals, hotels, and apartment buildings omit the fourth floor entirely — jumping from 3 to 5 — just as Western buildings sometimes skip 13. Avoid using 4 in important business names, pricing (¥444 for a product is particularly inauspicious), and addresses when alternatives exist. The combination 14 (sounds like "want to die"), 24 ("easy to die"), and 44 ("die die") are all strongly avoided.

Other Cautious Numbers

While 4 is the primary unlucky number, context matters for others. The number 5 (wǔ) sounds like "no" (否, fǒu) in some dialects, and 7 can carry associations with ghosts and the spirit world during the seventh lunar month. However, 5 and 7 are not universally unlucky — in BaZi, 5 relates to the central Earth element and can be powerful and stabilizing. Context, dialect, and elemental associations all modify meaning.

Lucky Colors and Their Elemental Roots

Red — Protection, Vitality, and Good Fortune

Red is the foundational lucky color in Chinese tradition. It represents Fire energy — warmth, vitality, passion, and the power to dispel negativity. Red is worn at weddings (the traditional Chinese wedding gown is red, not white), given in red envelopes (红包, hóng bāo) during New Year and celebrations, and hung at entrances during festivals to ward off misfortune. In feng shui, red activates the Fame and Reputation sector (South). For Fire Day Masters in BaZi, red is an excellent personal color; for Water Day Masters, red should be used sparingly as it represents a controlling element.

Gold and Yellow — Wealth, Imperial Power, and Earth Energy

Gold and yellow are the colors of Earth energy — stability, nourishment, and material accumulation. In imperial China, yellow was reserved exclusively for the emperor. Today, gold tones in home decor signal prosperity intention and are widely used in wealth-activation feng shui cures. Gold signifies not just money but the sustained, reliable wealth built through steady effort — the kind associated with Earth and Metal element types.

Green — Growth, Health, and Wood Energy

Green carries the expansive, vital energy of Wood — associated with spring, new beginnings, healing, and personal growth. In feng shui, green is ideal for the Health sector (East) and the Wealth sector (Southeast, which is also Wood element). For Wood Day Masters, green is an affirming personal color that reinforces their natural strengths. Jade — the most precious green stone in Chinese culture — carries Wood energy's gifts of protection, longevity, and prosperity.

Black and Dark Blue — Intelligence, Career, and Water Energy

Dark tones carry the depth and wisdom of Water energy. Black is the color of the Career sector (North bagua) and supports intellectual endeavors, strategic thinking, and professional advancement. Despite Western associations of black with mourning, in Chinese metaphysics it is a powerful, auspicious color for career and knowledge-seeking. Water Day Masters often find dark navy, charcoal, and black particularly supportive in professional settings.

White and Silver — Clarity, Precision, and Metal Energy

White and metallic silver represent Metal energy — purity, precision, and the capacity to cut through confusion. White is used in Metal-activating feng shui cures for the Helpful People (Northwest) and Creativity (West) sectors. Note that white is also associated with mourning in Chinese tradition, so it should be used thoughtfully in social contexts — brilliant in home decor and professional settings, but approached carefully in ceremonial gifts.

Practical tip: To activate prosperity in your life, combine 8 with gold/yellow and place both in the Southeast (Wealth) sector of your home. A bowl of eight gold coins or eight golden citrine crystals in the Southeast corner is one of the most traditional and widely used wealth activation cures in classical feng shui.

Applying Lucky Numbers and Colors in Daily Life

Start with the spaces you control most: your wallet (carry red inside, choose gold or black exteriors), your phone wallpaper (an image featuring your lucky element's color), your workspace (add a plant or green object in the East for health, a bright lamp in the South for recognition). When choosing business names or brand colors, consult the elemental associations of your industry — Fire industries (media, marketing) benefit from red and orange; Earth industries (real estate, food) from yellow and terracotta; Water industries (tech, research) from dark blue and black.

Numbers and colors are not magic — they are signals of intention. Using them consistently creates a coherent energetic environment that reinforces your goals, reminds you daily of what you are building toward, and aligns your immediate surroundings with the broader flow of good fortune.
