The Heart Sutra

Prajñāpāramitā Hṛdaya · The Heart of Perfect Wisdom

The shortest and most recited of the wisdom sutras. In about 260 characters it holds the whole teaching of emptiness — that nothing exists as a fixed, separate self.

🪷 A cultural & study reference. This translation is offered for reading and reflection. Wording of the original varies by edition; renderings below are plain-English and interpretive, not a definitive or liturgical text.
Full text

The sutra, complete 心經

Original text attributed to the Xuanzang translation.

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。舍利子，色不異空，空不異色，色即是空，空即是色，受想行識，亦復如是。舍利子，是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。是故空中無色，無受想行識，無眼耳鼻舌身意，無色聲香味觸法，無眼界，乃至無意識界，無無明，亦無無明盡，乃至無老死，亦無老死盡。無苦集滅道，無智亦無得。以無所得故，菩提薩埵，依般若波羅蜜多故，心無罣礙，無罣礙故，無有恐怖，遠離顛倒夢想，究竟涅槃。三世諸佛，依般若波羅蜜多故，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。故知般若波羅蜜多，是大神咒，是大明咒，是無上咒，是無等等咒，能除一切苦，真實不虛。

故說般若波羅蜜多咒，即說咒曰：
揭諦揭諦，波羅揭諦，波羅僧揭諦，菩提薩婆訶。
gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā

English, line by line

What each passage says

A plain-English rendering for study.

觀自在菩薩，行深般若波羅蜜多時，照見五蘊皆空，度一切苦厄。
Guān zìzài púsà, xíng shēn bōrě bōluómìduō shí…
The Bodhisattva of Compassion, deep in the practice of perfect wisdom, saw clearly that the five aggregates — form, feeling, perception, impulse, and awareness — are all empty of any fixed self, and so moved beyond all suffering.
色不異空，空不異色，色即是空，空即是色。
Sè bù yì kōng, kōng bù yì sè, sè jí shì kōng, kōng jí shì sè.
Form is not other than emptiness; emptiness is not other than form. Form itself is emptiness; emptiness itself is form. What appears and its open, interdependent nature are not two separate things.
是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。
Shì zhū fǎ kōng xiàng, bù shēng bù miè, bù gòu bù jìng, bù zēng bù jiǎn.
In this empty nature, all things: not born, not destroyed; not stained, not pure; not increasing, not decreasing. Nothing to grasp, nothing to lose.
心無罣礙，無罣礙故，無有恐怖，遠離顛倒夢想，究竟涅槃。
Xīn wú guà'ài… yuǎnlí diāndǎo mèngxiǎng, jiūjìng nièpán.
With the mind free of obstruction, there is no fear. Leaving behind upside-down dreams and distorted views, one arrives at complete peace.
揭諦揭諦，波羅揭諦，波羅僧揭諦，菩提薩婆訶。
gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā
Gone, gone, gone beyond, gone completely beyond — awakening, so be it. Traditionally left untranslated: the mantra is the whole sutra, distilled to sound.
Meaning

What the Heart Sutra is about

The one idea: emptiness

The whole sutra turns on one word — emptiness (空, śūnyatā). It does not mean "nothing exists." It means nothing exists on its own: every thing arises in relationship to everything else, and so has no fixed, separate self to cling to.

"Form is emptiness"

The most quoted line — 色即是空，空即是色 — says appearances (form) and their open, interdependent nature (emptiness) are two sides of one coin. You don't escape the world to find peace; you see the world clearly.

Why it eases fear

The sutra's promise is psychological: when the mind stops gripping fixed ideas of self and things, "there is no fear." It's a text about letting go of distortion — read by many simply as a daily reminder to loosen the grip of anxious, grasping thought.

The mantra

It closes with a mantra left in Sanskrit sound: gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā — "gone beyond." Tradition keeps it untranslated on purpose, treating the sound itself as the teaching's heart.

Cultural context

On reading & "merit"

The merit of study 功德

Buddhist tradition often speaks of the merit (gongde) of reading or copying a sutra — a cultural way of honoring the value of the teaching and the care one brings to it. Here that means simply: reading with a settled, sincere heart. It is a framework of intention, offered for reflection — not a promise of any particular outcome.
